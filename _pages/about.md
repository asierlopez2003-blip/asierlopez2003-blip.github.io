I am a physicist working at the boundary between theoretical modelling and the
instruments that test it. My degree is in Particle Physics and Cosmology
(University of Cantabria–CSIC–UIMP), and my research has moved between three
settings that share the same habit of working from data and simulation:
particle physics, observational astrophysics, and detector development.

**Where I work**

- **Observational astrophysics.** I characterise the morphology of AGN host
  galaxies using deep imaging from the Gran Telescopio Canarias and the
  Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope, with non-parametric measurements
  ([statmorph](https://www.statmorph.com/)) rather than visual classification.
  The question is whether the type 1 / type 2 distinction survives the evidence
  of past mergers.
- **Collider phenomenology.** Through a JAE fellowship at IFCA I work on double
  Higgs production and the trilinear Higgs couplings that the HL-LHC could
  constrain.
- **Detector development.** As a summer student in CERN's Solid State Detector
  group I characterise solid-state radiation detectors, measuring their
  current-voltage response and their charge-collection timing with the
  Transient Current Technique.

**What sits alongside the physics**

Much of my work is computational. As Computational Physics Lead and co-founder
of RadIA I build Monte Carlo simulations with Geant4-GATE for
radiation-matter interactions, and as Coordinator and Founder of the EUNICE AI
for Science Club I work on bringing AI methods into physics research practice
rather than treating them as a separate activity. I have also worked inside a
clinical setting, reviewing and optimising 95 Radixact treatment plans in
RayStation at the Hospital Universitario Marqués de Valdecilla — an experience
that shaped how seriously I take the downstream consequences of a model.
